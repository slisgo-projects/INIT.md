# AI Work Intensification

**Date:** 2026-10-02
**Status:** current
**Provenance:** findings drawn from web-search tool result summaries (2026-10-02), not from the source pages; no page was opened. Quotes, figures, and sample sizes are second-hand and unverified against originals.

## Question

- Do AI productivity gains reduce human workload, or expand scope and ambition so that cognitive demand holds or rises?
- Is the compulsive pull of agentic tools ("drug of realizable potential") a documented pattern?

## Findings

- **Intensification, not relief.** Berkeley Haas 8-month ethnography, ~200 employees, one US tech firm (Ranganathan & Ye, HBR, 2026-02-09): workers sped up, broadened task scope, extended work into off-hours — mostly unasked. Driver: AI made "doing more" feel possible and intrinsically rewarding. Outcomes: cognitive fatigue, burnout, weaker decisions.
- **Jevons paradox applied to cognition.** Cheaper output raises demand for output; total work volume grows. Widely invoked 2026 framing (elezea, MindStudio, Unhustle).
- **Red Queen / ratchet.** Each gain becomes the new baseline; competitors and peers match it; standards reset upward (Wharton Knowledge, "AI efficiency trap"). Competitive markets convert efficiency into output, not slack.
- **Compulsion loop.** Agentic coding described as a slot machine: success = dopamine, failure = adrenaline; hard to stop; sleep disruption (Yegge, "The AI Vampire," 2026-02; Axios 2026-04-04; LeadDev; quent.in). Yegge: "The better you get at it, the more you want to use it"; recommends ≤3–5 h/day agent work.
- **Oversight is the costly part.** BCG study, 1,488 US workers (HBR 2026, "AI brain fry"): high-oversight AI use is the most fatiguing form; perceived productivity rises to ~3 tools, then dips. Engineering brain-fry rate 17%.
- **Solo-operator specific.** Every agent stream draws on one working memory; role switching every 30–60 min is the operating model, not an edge case (Graymatter). AI amplifies the operator's vision but does not challenge it.
- **Reference docs in `reference/` corroborate:** Seramount (judgment as bottleneck; "most productive with AI" workers report more burnout); Effectus (absorption bottleneck; elasticity point; follow-up post on cognitive speed limit not found online).
- **Unverified secondary claim:** NBER finding of ~3 extra hours/week in AI-exposed occupations — cited only via search-engine summary of Jevons-paradox coverage; no primary or named secondary source located.
- **Not found:** the YouTube video on scope growth under competition.

## Implications for the project

- Team-of-one targeting concentrates every absorption cost on one human; the schema reduces reload cost but does not cap ambition or session length.
- Candidate MOTIVATION.md strand: intensification and compulsion as a known limit of the team-of-one model. Elicit from operator; do not ghost-write.
- Candidate `ancillary/HUMAN.md` material: stopping rules, session caps, ambition-vs-absorption checks.
- Candidate schema question (open, not decided): whether INIT.md should carry any absorption-limit mechanism, or leave it to HUMAN.md.
